Most lesson blocks help students take in an idea. Practice blocks ask them to **use it**: explain it in their own words, answer the hard lines they will hear from clients, or make the calls in a realistic case. Feedback is instant, there are no scores or grades, and nothing a student writes or says is shown to you.

You find all three in the **Practice** group of the block menu. Type `/` in a lesson and search for **explain**, **drill**, or **scenario**.

<Note>
  Practice blocks work in lessons only. They need a signed-in student, so they are not available on your academy's Home page or on offer landing pages.
</Note>

## The three blocks

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Explain it back" icon="message-circle">
    The student explains an idea in their own words, typed or spoken, and sees which of your key points they covered.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Rapid-fire drill" icon="zap">
    Your hardest lines, such as client objections, come one at a time. The student answers fast and sees whether the answer would land.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Decision scenario" icon="signpost">
    A short branching case. The student makes the calls, sees what each choice leads to, and reaches an ending.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Explain it back

Use it right after the passage that teaches an idea worth being able to say out loud, for example "explain to a client why your method has a name".

- **You write** the prompt, an optional audience ("a client who thinks naming things is marketing fluff"), and two to six **key points** a good explanation covers. Each key point can carry a short hint.
- **The student** types an explanation, or records one of up to two minutes that is transcribed and can be edited before checking. They then see their answer, one line of feedback (it lands, or the one idea that would make it land), and your key points with the covered ones ticked. They can rewrite it any time.
- **Settings:** choose whether students answer by **Type or speak**, **Type only**, or **Speak only**.

### Rapid-fire drill

Use it for pushback your students will actually face: objections on a sales call, tough questions from a board, a client who disagrees.

- **You write** a title, a short setup, who is speaking ("Prospect"), and three to eight **lines**. For each line you add one to three points a strong answer makes, and a model answer.
- **The student** gets the lines one at a time and answers by typing or speaking. After each answer they see **Strong**, **Getting there**, or **Missed it**, with each point ticked or missed and your model answer behind **Show a strong answer**. The round ends with a short summary and **Run the misses again**.
- **Settings:** a per-line **Timer** (a nudge, not a cutoff), how students answer, and the speaker label.

### Decision scenario

Use it when the lesson teaches judgment under trade-offs, where the right call depends on the situation.

- **You write** a title, a setup, up to ten steps made of **decisions** (two to four choices each, every choice with a consequence) and **endings** (great, mixed, or poor, each with a takeaway). You can start from **Draft with AI**: describe the situation in a line, and a first draft is built from your lesson for you to review and edit.
- **The student** reads the situation, makes each call, sees what it leads to, and reaches an ending that reflects the path they took, with a recap of their choices. They can try another path.
- Everything in a scenario is your own writing, so no AI runs while students play.

## Setting up a practice block

<Steps>
  <Step title="Insert the block">
    In a lesson, type `/` and choose **Explain it back**, **Rapid-fire drill**, or **Decision scenario** from the **Practice** group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write it on the card">
    Fill in the prompt, key points, or drill lines directly on the block. The card in the editor looks the way students will see it. For a decision scenario, write the title and setup on the card, then click **Edit scenario** to build its decisions and endings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Adjust its settings">
    Select the block and its settings open in the **settings panel** on the right side of the editor. On smaller screens, use the block's settings button instead (a scenario opens in a side sheet). See [Block editor](/content/block-editor#block-settings-panel).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  You can also ask the lesson assistant or your connected AI tool to add a practice block, for example "add a drill with the five objections from this lesson". It writes the block in the same format you would.
</Tip>

## What you see as the owner

Open a course's insights (the chart icon in the course editor, or a course in **Insights**) and choose the **Practice** tab. It appears once a course has practice blocks.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Explanations" icon="message-circle">
    How many students covered every key point, how often each key point is covered, and how many tries it took.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Drills" icon="zap">
    For each line, the share of students whose latest answer is strong, and the point that gets missed most.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Scenarios" icon="signpost">
    Where first runs end, the choices students make at each decision, and how many reach a great ending.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

When something stands out, such as a key point few students cover or a weak choice most of them make, the tab says so in a sentence at the top. That is usually the part of the lesson to strengthen.

## Privacy: signals, not answers

<Info>
  You see signals, never your students' words. Explanations, drill answers, recordings, and the path a student takes through a scenario stay private to that student. Your Practice tab shows totals across students, such as how many covered a key point or picked a choice, never one student's answers or path. Students see a note on every practice block saying that only they see what they write.
</Info>

The results only count students enrolled in the course, so your own test runs and your team's stay out of the numbers.

## AI credits

- Checking an explanation or a drill answer **uses no AI credits**. Neither does transcribing a spoken answer.
- A decision scenario runs **no AI** while students play.
- **Draft with AI** in a decision scenario uses **1 credit** per draft.
